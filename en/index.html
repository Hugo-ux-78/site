About

Care without walls

I have been a nurse since 2000. In 2017 I joined the team that designed the Case Management Programme for People with Complex Chronic Disease at the Litoral Alentejano Local Health Unit (ULSLA), in Portugal.

I started in cardiology at Santa Cruz Hospital, then worked at Curry Cabral Hospital, the Instituto do Coração and in home care in Almada. In Odemira I led the Integrated Continuing Care Team. I hold a master’s degree and specialist title in Medical-Surgical Nursing (people with chronic conditions) from the Lisbon School of Nursing (ESEL).

I was a member of ULSLA’s Integrated Care Unit and I am its internal telehealth champion. In November 2026 I joined the management board of the pilot project of its High Performance Centre for Complex Chronic Disease (CED DCC) as coordinating nurse.

2nd National Congress on Health and Environment · 2026

Care and environmental impact of a decentralised blood collection point

Poster presented at the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation, Lisbon, 8–10 April 2026.

Read the abstract

At the Odemira Health Centre, in a large, ageing area with poor public transport, ULSLA brought hospital care to the community through three integrated services: the decentralised blood collection point, with 7,185 collection requests in 2025, an average of 37 collections a day and collections at home and in care homes for the most vulnerable people; cardiopulmonary tests (ECG, ambulatory blood pressure monitoring, Holter and spirometry); and internal medicine outpatient clinics. Decentralisation avoided trips to the Litoral Alentejano Hospital, saved fuel and reduced CO₂ emissions. According to the study’s estimate, over two years the collection point helped avoid more than half a million kilometres of travel. Conclusion: a closer, fairer and more sustainable National Health Service is possible.
